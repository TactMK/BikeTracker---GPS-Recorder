Bike Tracker Privacy Policy

Last updated: 18 November 2026. Applies to version 2.0 and later.

Bike Tracker is an iPhone app that records bike rides. Its name under the icon is “Bike”, and earlier versions were called “BikeTracker - GPS Recorder”. The app is published on the App Store by Ilya Klimenko, an individual developer, who is responsible for the data described here. This policy explains what data the app uses, why, and what choices you have.

Summary

  • You do not need an account, and the developer does not run a server.
  • Your rides are stored on your iPhone and, if you use iCloud, in your own iCloud.
  • The app does not send your location or routes to the developer or to anyone else.
  • Usage analytics is turned off by default. It works only if you turn it on.
  • The app has no advertising, does not track you across other companies’ apps and websites, and does not sell data.

Data stored on your device and in iCloud

The app stores your rides on your device. A ride includes its route, speed, distance, altitude and time, and the readings of any sensors you connect, such as cadence, heart rate and power. The app also stores your bikes, their photos and their service records. Photos you take during a ride are saved to your photo library, and the app keeps a reference to them.

If iCloud is turned on for the app, this data is synced through your private iCloud database. That database is operated by Apple, and the developer has no access to it.

Your settings and the details you enter under “About you” (weight, year of birth, sex, maximum heart rate and FTP) are stored only on your device.

Permissions

The app asks for a permission only when a feature needs it:

  • Location (While Using the App). Used to record the route, speed and distance of a ride, including when the screen is off during a recording. The app never asks for “Always” access. It uses location only while the record screen is open or a ride is being recorded.
  • Motion & Fitness. Used to read the barometer for altitude and elevation gain.
  • Bluetooth. Used to connect the speed, cadence, heart rate and power sensors you pair. The app asks for it when you start a search for sensors.
  • Camera. Used to take a photo of your bike or a photo during a ride.
  • Photos. Used to save the photos you take during a ride to your library and to show them with that ride.
  • Notifications. Used for reminders created on your device: a service that is due, a ride that is paused while you are moving, and the end of a free trial. No notification is sent from a server.
  • Apple Health. Described in the next section.

You can refuse any permission or withdraw it later in the iOS Settings app. Location is the only one the app cannot record a ride without.

Apple Health

With your permission, the app does the following:

  • It saves your rides to Health as cycling workouts, with duration, distance, speed, cadence, heart rate, power, active energy and route. You can turn this off in Settings, under Apple Health.
  • It reads cycling workouts, so that your history and totals can include rides recorded by other apps and devices.
  • It reads your weight, year of birth and sex, but only when you tap “Fill in from Health”. These values are used to calculate calories, and you can type them in yourself instead.

Data read from Apple Health stays on your device. It is not stored in iCloud, not included in analytics, not shared with anyone, and not used for advertising or marketing.

Analytics

The app includes an analytics tool provided by Amplitude, Inc. It is turned off by default. Nothing is collected unless you turn on “Recording quality data” in Settings, and you can turn it off again at any time. Ride recording and all paid features work the same whether it is on or off.

When analytics is on, the app sends the following to Amplitude:

  • Which screens and buttons of the app are used, including the purchase screen: the plan you select and whether a purchase is completed.
  • A few app settings: units, the layout of the record screen, and whether Apple Health is connected.
  • For each saved or imported ride, information about recording quality: gaps in the GPS signal, what measured the ride (GPS or a wheel sensor), and the ride’s distance, elevation gain, top speed, moving time and stopped time.
  • The type of bike and the types of sensors that were connected, without their names.
  • Technical information: app version, iOS version, device model, language, region setting, Low Power Mode, the date you first opened the app, and the daily performance figures that iOS reports for the app.
  • Two technical identifiers: one for the device, and one shared between your own devices through your iCloud. They contain no name, email address or Apple ID. They are used to count the events of one rider together.

The app never sends the following to analytics: coordinates, routes, the start point or the area of a ride; your name, email address or Apple ID; an advertising identifier; the names of your bikes, sensors or files; workouts or body measurements from Apple Health; your weight, year of birth or sex.

The app instructs Amplitude not to record your IP address and not to use it to determine your city or region. As with any internet connection, the receiving server can see the IP address while the data is being delivered.

This data is used only to find and fix recording problems and to understand which parts of the app are used. It is not combined with data from other companies and is not used for advertising.

Legal basis

Analytics is based on your consent, which you give by turning the switch on and withdraw by turning it off. Data stored on your device and in your iCloud is processed to provide the app’s features at your request.

Who receives data

  • Apple. Apple provides iCloud, Apple Health, Apple Maps (the maps shown in the app) and App Store purchases, each under Apple’s own privacy policy.
  • Amplitude, Inc. If you turn analytics on, Amplitude processes the analytics data described above for the developer, on servers in the United States. Amplitude acts as a service provider and may not use the data for its own purposes. Its privacy notice is available at amplitude.com/privacy.

The developer does not share data with anyone else. Data would be disclosed only if the law required it.

Purchases

Bike Tracker Premium is purchased through the App Store. Apple handles the payment. The developer does not receive your name, payment details or Apple ID.

Export, import and diagnostics

A ride is exported as a GPX or FIT file only when you choose to export it, and it is sent only where you choose. A diagnostics report is created only when you request one in Settings, and you decide whether to share it. Files you import are read on your device.

How long data is kept, and how to delete it

  • Rides are kept until you delete them. You can delete a single ride, or use “Delete All Data” in Settings to remove all rides from your device and your iCloud.
  • Deleting the app removes the data it stored on your device, except the technical identifier and the date of first launch, which iOS keeps in the keychain. You can manage app data in iCloud in the iOS Settings app.
  • Workouts saved to Apple Health are managed in the Health app.
  • Turning analytics off stops collection immediately and deletes any analytics data that has not yet been sent.
  • “Delete All Data” also resets the analytics identifiers, so later analytics is not connected to earlier analytics.
  • Analytics data that was already sent is kept only as long as it is needed to improve the app. You can ask for it to be deleted at any time by writing to the address below.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access your data, to correct it, to delete it, to receive a copy of it, to restrict or object to its use, and to withdraw your consent. Most of your data is on your own device and in your own iCloud, where you control it directly. For analytics data, you can make a request by writing to the address below. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with your local data protection authority.

Security

Data on your device and in iCloud is protected by iOS and by your Apple Account. Analytics data is sent over an encrypted connection.

Children

The app is not intended for children under 13. Analytics stays off unless it is turned on in Settings.
